> [!abstract] About This Note
> Understanding common attack methods helps security professionals identify, prevent, and respond to threats. This note covers phishing, malware, and social engineering — the most frequently encountered attack categories.

---

# Phishing

> [!info] Definition
> Phishing is the use of digital communication to trick people into revealing sensitive information or installing malicious software.

| Attack | Description |
| ------ | ----------- |
| **Business Email Compromise (BEC)** | Fake business email requesting sensitive information or money |
| **Spear Phishing** | Targeted phishing against a specific individual or group |
| **Whaling** | Spear phishing aimed at executives or high-level employees |
| **Vishing** | Voice call used to steal sensitive information |
| **Smishing** | SMS or text message phishing |

---

# Malware

> [!info] Definition
> Malicious software designed to damage systems, steal information, or gain unauthorized access.

| Malware | Behaviour |
|---------|-----------|
| **Virus** | Requires user interaction to spread (e.g., opening a malicious file) |
| **Worm** | Self-replicates and spreads automatically across networks |
| **Ransomware** | Encrypts files and demands payment to restore access |
| **Spyware** | Secretly collects user information without consent |

> [!note] Virus vs. Worm — Key Distinction
> A **virus** needs the user to do something (open a file, click a link). A **worm** spreads on its own — no user action required.

---

# Social Engineering

> [!info] Definition
> The manipulation of people into revealing confidential information or performing actions that compromise security.

| Attack | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Social Media Phishing** | Uses information gathered from social media to target victims |
| **Watering Hole Attack** | Compromises a website frequently visited by the target group |
| **USB Baiting** | Leaves infected USB drives for victims to plug into computers |
| **Physical Social Engineering** | Impersonates trusted personnel to gain physical access |

---

## Why Social Engineering Works

> [!quote] The weakest link in any security system is the human element.

| Principle | How It's Exploited |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Authority** | Victims trust people in positions of power |
| **Intimidation** | Fear or pressure forces quick compliance |
| **Consensus** | "Everyone else already did it" |
| **Scarcity** | Fear of missing a limited opportunity |
| **Familiarity** | A fake personal connection lowers guard |
| **Trust** | Confidence built over time before exploiting |
| **Urgency** | Pressure to act fast without thinking |

---

# Attack Comparison

| Category | Primary Goal | Classic Example |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Phishing | Trick users into giving up information | Fake Microsoft login email |
| Malware | Damage, steal from, or control systems | Ransomware encryption |
| Social Engineering | Manipulate human behaviour | Fake IT support call |

# Exam Tips

> [!tip] Quick Recall
> - **Virus** → needs user action &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Worm** → spreads automatically
> - **Ransomware** → encrypts your data
> - **Spyware** → secretly watches you
> - **BEC** → targets businesses via email
> - **Whaling** → targets executives specifically
> - **Vishing** → voice &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Smishing** → SMS
