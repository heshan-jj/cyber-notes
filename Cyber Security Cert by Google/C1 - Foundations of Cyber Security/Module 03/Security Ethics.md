> **Security ethics** are guidelines for making appropriate decisions as a security professional. It requires remaining unbiased and maintaining the security and confidentiality of private data.

# Counterattacks: Laws and Ethics

> [!warning] The Rule on Counterattacks
> Counterattacking in cybersecurity is generally illegal or heavily restricted. Security professionals are advised to **only defend**.

## U.S. Standpoint on Counterattacks

In the United States, deploying a counterattack on a threat actor is **illegal**.

*   **Key Laws:** Computer Fraud and Abuse Act of 1986, Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act of 2015.
*   **Vigilantism:** Counterattacking is perceived as an act of vigilantism (a person who is not law enforcement deciding to stop a crime).
*   **Risks:** 
    *   Escalation causing more damage.
    *   International implications (e.g., if the threat actor is a state-sponsored hacktivist).
*   **Exceptions:** Only approved employees of the federal government or military personnel can legally counterattack.

## International Standpoint (ICJ)

The **International Court of Justice (ICJ)** states that a counterattack is permissible *only* if **all** of the following strictly apply:

1.  It only affects the party that attacked first.
2.  It is a direct communication asking the initial attacker to stop.
3.  It does not escalate the situation.
4.  The effects can be reversed.

> [!note] Why Organizations Don't Counterattack
> Organizations typically do not counterattack because the ICJ parameters are extremely hard to measure, and the negative outcomes (like escalation) are difficult to control. For more international guidelines, refer to the **Tallinn Manual**.

# Ethical Principles & Methodologies

To address the limitations on counterattacks, the industry relies on frameworks and controls (like the CIA Triad) to maintain privacy and follow the law.

| Concept | Definition & Ethical Obligation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Confidentiality** | Only authorized users can access specific assets/data. *Obligation:* Maintain a high level of respect for privacy to safeguard data. |
| **Privacy Protection** | Safeguarding personal information from unauthorized use. Includes **PII** (Personally Identifiable Information) and **SPII** (Sensitive PII). *Obligation:* Secure information, identify vulnerabilities, and manage risks. |
| **Laws** | Rules recognized and enforced by a governing entity (e.g., **HIPAA** for protecting Health Information/PHI). *Obligation:* Protect the organization and people while remaining unbiased, transparent, and legally compliant. |

## Core Duties of a Security Professional

To uphold these principles, you must:
*   Remain unbiased, honest, responsible, and respect the law.
*   Be transparent, just, and rely on evidence.
*   Stay consistently invested in your work to ethically address issues.
*   Stay informed and continuously advance your skills.
