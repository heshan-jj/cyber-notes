# Core Concepts

## The CIA Triad

The **confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA) triad** is a model that helps inform how organizations consider risk when setting up systems and security policies.

> [!note] CIA Principles
> CIA are the three foundational principles used by cybersecurity professionals to establish appropriate controls that mitigate threats, risks, and vulnerabilities.

---

## Controls, Frameworks, and Compliance

They work together to ensure security goals and processes are implemented correctly and meet regulatory requirements.

| Concept | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Security Controls** | Safeguards designed to reduce specific security risks. |
| **Security Frameworks** | Guidelines used for building plans to help mitigate risks and threats to data and privacy. See [[Frameworks and Controls]]. |
| **Compliance** | The process of adhering to internal standards and external regulations. |

### Security Framework Components
Frameworks have four core components:
1. Identifying and documenting security goals
2. Setting guidelines to achieve security goals
3. Implementing strong security processes
4. Monitoring and communicating results

# Specific Frameworks and Regulations

> [!info] The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)
> A U.S.-based agency that develops multiple voluntary compliance frameworks that organizations worldwide can use to help manage risk. The more aligned an organization is with compliance, the lower the risk. 
> *Examples: NIST Cybersecurity Framework ([[NIST CSF Core Functions|CSF]]), NIST Risk Management Framework ([[NIST Risk Management Framework|RMF]]).*

| Framework / Regulation | Focus | Description |
|------------------------|-------|-------------|
| **FERC-NERC** | Power Grid | Regulation for organizations working with electricity or the U.S. and North American power grid. Requires adhering to Critical Infrastructure Protection (CIP) Reliability Standards. |
| **FedRAMP®** | Cloud Services | U.S. federal government program that standardizes security assessment, authorization, monitoring, and handling of cloud services. |
| **CIS®** | Defense Planning | Center for Internet Security. A nonprofit providing controls to safeguard systems/networks against attacks and actionable controls for security incidents. |
| **GDPR** | E.U. Data Privacy | General Data Protection Regulation. Protects E.U. residents' data and privacy rights. Organizations have 72 hours to notify citizens of a breach. |
| **PCI DSS** | Credit Cards | Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard. Ensures secure environments for storing, accepting, processing, and transmitting credit card info to reduce fraud. |
| **HIPAA** | Healthcare | Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (1996). Protects patients' health information (PHI) based on Privacy, Security, and Breach notification rules. |
| **ISO** | International Standards | International Organization for Standardization. Establishes standards related to technology, manufacturing, and management across borders. |
| **SOC Type 1 & 2** | User Access & Audits | System and Organizations Controls. Developed by AICPA to assess an organization’s financial compliance and risk levels regarding user access, confidentiality, and data safety. |

> [!tip] Pro Tip
> There are a number of regulations that are frequently revised. You are encouraged to keep up-to-date with changes and explore more frameworks, controls, and compliance. Two suggestions to research: the **Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act** and the **Sarbanes-Oxley Act**.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Controls, frameworks, and compliance work together to help organizations maintain a low level of risk.
- As a security analyst, it’s important to stay up-to-date on common frameworks, controls, and compliance regulations and be aware of changes to the cybersecurity landscape to help ensure the safety of both organizations and people.
