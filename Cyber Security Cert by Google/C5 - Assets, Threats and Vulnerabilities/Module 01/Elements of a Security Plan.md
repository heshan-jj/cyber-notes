> [!abstract] Elements of a Security Plan
> Security is a **culture** built on **people, processes, and technology**. To prepare for risks, organizations communicate and implement their security plans through three foundational pillars: **Policies** (strategic rules), **Standards** (tactical benchmarks), and **Procedures** (operational step-by-step instructions).

---

# Security as a Culture

Security extends far beyond IT departments — it is a shared set of values across every level of an organization, from internal employees to external vendors and customers.

> [!note] The People-Centric Approach
> Effective security plans prioritize **people**. Accounting for diverse backgrounds, technical comfort levels, and potential human errors ensures broad organizational alignment and participation.

### Common Risk Factors in Security Plans
Security plans organize and address risks related to the **damage, disclosure, or loss** of information caused by:
- **Human error:** Unintentional slips such as emailing sensitive files via personal accounts.
- **Attacks:** Malicious attempts by external or internal threat actors.
- **System malfunctions & physical damage:** Hardware crashes, environmental hazards, or facility failures.

# The Three Elements of a Security Plan

Organizations communicate their security posture using three distinct, interrelated layers:

```
               ┌────────────────────────┐
               │        POLICIES        │  ◄── Strategic (What & Why)
               └───────────┬────────────┘
                           │ Informs
               ┌───────────▼────────────┐
               │       STANDARDS        │  ◄── Tactical (How Well / Benchmarks)
               └───────────┬────────────┘
                           │ Implements
               ┌───────────▼────────────┐
               │       PROCEDURES       │  ◄── Operational (How-To Step-by-Step)
               └────────────────────────┘
```

---

## 1. Policies (Strategic)

> [!info] Definition
> A **policy** is a formal set of rules that reduces risk and protects information assets. Policies serve as the foundation of any security program.

- **Role:** High-level strategic guidance answering: *"What are we protecting, and why?"*
- **Scope:** Defines boundaries, objectives, acceptable behavior, and consequences of violations.
- **Example:** **Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)** — Signed by new hires to establish clear boundaries for accessing organizational systems and data.

---

## 2. Standards (Tactical)

> [!info] Definition
> **Standards** are measurable benchmarks and reference points used to inform and shape policies.

- **Role:** Tactical guidance defining *"How well"* an organization protects assets to maintain consistency and compliance.
- **Scope:** Sets mandatory specifications, baseline configurations, or industry-aligned minimum thresholds.
- **Example:** Mandating password complexity based on **NIST SP 800-63B** guidelines (e.g., specifying passwords must be at least 8 characters long).

---

## 3. Procedures (Operational)

> [!info] Definition
> **Procedures** are detailed, step-by-step instructions for executing a specific security task.

- **Role:** Practical, actionable directions answering: *"How do we perform this specific action?"*
- **Scope:** Promotes consistency, accountability, and operational efficiency across the organization.
- **Examples:**
  - Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for securely resetting a locked account.
  - Instructions on creating and managing secure passphrases.

# Core Comparison at a Glance

| Element | Level | Focus Question | Function | Real-World Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Policy** | Strategic | *What* and *Why*? | Establishes organizational rules & goals | Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) |
| **Standard** | Tactical | *How well*? | Defines quantifiable baselines & benchmarks | NIST SP 800-63B (Password Requirements) |
| **Procedure** | Operational | *How to do it*? | Step-by-step task instructions | Step-by-step guide to reset a locked password |

---

# Exam Tips

> [!tip] Key Distinctions
> - **Policy** = The Rule (*What & Why*) → High-level, mandatory rules set by leadership.
> - **Standard** = The Benchmark (*How Well*) → Measurable reference metric (e.g., NIST, ISO).
> - **Procedure** = The Recipe (*Step-by-Step*) → Detailed instructions to execute the standard.
> - **Security Triad:** Security relies equally on **People**, **Processes**, and **Technology**.
